# Mentoring & Growth

---

## Q1: How do you mentor and develop junior/mid-level engineers?

**Answer:**

My mentoring approach has three pillars:

1. **Structured onboarding** — I built an AI-powered onboarding agent that helps new team members understand the codebase, architecture, and workflows interactively. This reduces ramp-up time and frees senior engineers from repetitive Q&A.
2. **PR reviews as teaching moments** — I don't just approve/reject. I leave detailed comments explaining *why* something should change, link to relevant docs, and suggest patterns.
3. **Gradual ownership** — I assign progressively complex tasks, starting with well-scoped bug fixes, then features, then design ownership. I stay available for guidance but let them drive.

---

## Q2: Give an example of how you helped someone grow technically.

**Answer (STAR):**

- **Situation:** A mid-level engineer on the team was strong in feature development but struggled with production debugging and RCA.
- **Task:** Help them develop debugging skills to operate independently.
- **Action:** During the next production issue, instead of jumping in myself, I pair-debugged with them. I walked through my methodology: start with logs/metrics, form hypotheses, trace the request flow, check infrastructure when application-level clues are exhausted. I let them drive the keyboard while I guided the approach.
- **Result:** Within a couple of months, they were leading RCA investigations independently and even documented their own debugging runbook for the team.

## Q3: How do you create a culture of learning in your team?

**Answer:**

- **Hackathons** — I organized hackathon events where engineers could explore new technologies and ideas outside their daily work. This builds skills and team bonding.
- **R&R (Rewards & Recognition)** — I drive recognition programs to celebrate technical achievements, not just feature delivery.
- **Knowledge sharing** — I create architecture documents, RCA writeups, and design decision records that serve as learning material for the team.
- **Tool adoption** — I championed AI tooling (like GitHub Copilot) by demonstrating productivity gains, then helping the team integrate it into their workflow.

---

## Q4: How do you balance mentoring others with your own delivery responsibilities?

**Answer:**

I see mentoring as a force multiplier, not a distraction from delivery. Specifically:

- **Invest upfront, save later** — Time spent teaching debugging methodology to a team member means they can handle future incidents independently
- **Automation as mentoring** — Building the onboarding AI agent was both a delivery (tooling) and a mentoring investment
- **Async mentoring** — Detailed PR reviews, architecture docs, and decision records are scalable mentoring — they help the whole team without real-time cost
- **Prioritize based on impact** — If a deadline is tight, I'll handle the critical path myself and mentor on the less time-sensitive work
