# Job Interview — Behavioral (STAR Format)

Behavioral questions commonly asked in job interviews. Answers are templated — personalize with your specific examples.

---

## Q1: Why are you looking for a change?

**Answer:**

Focus on **pull factors** (what you're moving toward), not push factors:

- "I'm looking for an opportunity to work on larger-scale distributed systems and take on broader architectural responsibilities."
- "I want to be in an environment where I can have more impact on technology strategy and mentor a larger team."
- "I'm excited about [specific domain/tech at target company] and see a strong alignment with my experience in building scalable backend systems."

> **Never say:** Negative things about current employer, manager, or team.

---

## Q2: Tell me about yourself. (The 2-minute pitch)

**Answer template:**

```
[Current role] → [Experience summary] → [Key strengths] → [Why this opportunity]
```

"I'm a Lead Software Engineer with 8+ years of experience building full-stack applications in Java, Spring Boot, and React on AWS. I've led teams through complex technical challenges — from designing caching architectures for high-throughput APIs to debugging cross-service production issues at the network level. I'm passionate about mentoring engineers and driving engineering productivity — I've built AI-powered tools for integration testing and developer onboarding. I'm looking to take on a senior technical leadership role where I can influence architecture decisions at a broader scale."

---

## Q3: What's your biggest technical achievement?

**Answer (template):**

Pick one that shows: **technical depth + business impact + leadership**

Example: Redis caching design, VPC peering RCA, or AI agent for testing — whichever resonates with the target role.

Use full STAR format with quantified results where possible.

---

## Q4: Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?

**Answer:**

"I see myself as a principal/staff engineer driving architecture and technology strategy for a platform or product area. I want to be the person teams come to for solving complex distributed systems problems, and I want to build a culture of engineering excellence through mentoring and tooling."
